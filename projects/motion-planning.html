Motion planning for autonomous vehicles.

Making an F1TENTH RoboRacer plan faster, choose better paths, and work on physical hardware.

The problem

Sampling-based motion planners can spend too much time evaluating paths that are unlikely to produce a fast lap. This thesis explored how to make the sampling process more aware of the track and the car's likely apexes.

The work

I refactored the planner using SIMD vectorization, formulated a differentiable spatial sampling method, and integrated it into an end-to-end differentiable driving stack with model-predictive control.

  • Built modular ROS2 nodes that could switch between simulation and physical hardware.
  • Deployed the stack on an NVIDIA Jetson and validated it on a RoboRacer chassis.
  • Used an OptiTrack motion-capture system for live hardware inference on a circuit.
